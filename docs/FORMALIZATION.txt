2. INDEPENDENT DEFINITIONS AND THEIR IMPLEMENTATION BRIDGES

FidelityLean/Spec.lean imports Mathlib, not the implementation modules. In the
namespace Fidelity.Spec it defines the paper's objects and proposition types.
The assertions defined with "def ... : Prop" are specifications, not axioms
or proofs. FidelityLean/ZeroParameterSpec.lean imports this specification and
adds the seven zero-parameter proposition types.

The paper's symbolic field is represented by

  ParameterField n = FractionRing (MvPolynomial (Fin (n + 1)) Q).

Slot zero is the indeterminate t, or tau in the square-root cases. The other
slots are the n independent variables; symbolicX packages them as units.
Theorems prove that these indeterminates are nonzero. Lean's Fin indices begin
at zero, while paperIndex i = i.val + 1 gives the paper's indices 1,...,k.
All coefficient and affine-shift indices are integers.

The seven Weyl objects are defined independently of their affine determinants.
For a signed permutation w, let u_a be x_(sigma(a)) or its inverse according
to the sign at output position a. Put q=t except in BC and Bminus, where
q=tau^2. The specification P is the finite sum of

  [product_a u_a^(-k) rho_c(q,tau,u_a)]
  [product_(a<b) ((q u_a-u_b)(1-q u_a u_b)) /
                    ((u_a-u_b)(1-u_a u_b))]

over all signed permutations, divided by V_n(q) nu_c(n,k,q,tau), where

  V_n(q) = product_(r=1,...,n) (1 + q + ... + q^(r-1)).

The boundary factors and width-zero normalizations are:

  Case     rho_c(q,tau,z)             nu_c(n,0,q,tau)
  C0       1/(1-z^2)                  1
  Ct       (1-q z^2)/(1-z^2)          product_(r=1,...,n) (1+q^r)
  BC       (1+tau z)/(1-z^2)          1
  B0       1/(1-z)                    1
  Bminus   (1+tau z)/(1-z)            product_(r=0,...,n-1) (1-tau^(2r+1))
  Bplus    1                          product_(r=0,...,n-1) (1+q^r)
  Bt       (1-q z)/(1-z)              product_(r=1,...,n) (1+q^r)

For positive k the factor nu_c is 1. The exact definitions are P, parameter,
rho, nuZero, nu, Vn, weylPoint, weylPairProduct and weylSummand in Spec.lean.

The generating polynomials and their coefficients are independently defined:

  Edot(z)  = product_a (1+x_a z)(1+x_a^(-1) z),
  Eddot(z) = (1+z) Edot(z).

dotCoeff x r is the coefficient of z^r in Edot for 0<=r<=2n, and is zero
outside that interval. ddotCoeff has the analogous cutoff 0<=r<=2n+1.
Their coefficient conventions retain e_(2n-r)=e_r,
ee_(2n+1-r)=ee_r and ee_r=e_r+e_(r-1), including the zero extensions.
Writing these as e_r and ee_r and using one-based i, the four row kernels are

  lambda_i(m) = e_(n-i+m) - e_(n+i+m),
  h_i(m)      = e_(n-i+m) + e_(n+i+m-1),
  hPlus_i(m)  = e_(n-i+m) + e_(n+i+m-2),
  hHat_i(m)   = ee_(n-i+m) + ee_(n+i+m-1).

For a period K and weights w_j(y), JK is the sum over y in Z^k of

  det_(i,j=1,...,k) [w_j(y_i) row_i(j-K y_i)].

JKEven restricts this sum to Even(sum_i y_i). It does not require each y_i
to be even. The code uses finsum and proves finite support for the relevant
integrands; no arbitrary numerical truncation is substituted for the sum.

In these conventions, the seven main affine interfaces have the following
data. Each exponent below is an integer, j ranges from 1 to k, and y is an
integer shift.

  Case     K       row      w_j(y)                         prefactor/lattice
  B0       2k+1    h        (-1)^y t^[k y^2+(y^2+y)/2-j y]  1, all Z^k
  Bminus   2k      h        tau^[2k y^2-(2j-1)y]             1, all Z^k
  Bplus    2k      hPlus    (-1)^y t^[k y^2-(j-1)y]          1/2, all Z^k
  C0       2k+2    lambda   t^[(k+1)y^2-j y]                1, all Z^k
  Ct       2k      lambda   t^[k y^2-j y]                    1, even total sum
  BC       2k+1    lambda   (-1)^y tau^[(2k+1)y^2-2j y]      1, all Z^k
  Bt       2k      hHat     (-1)^y t^[k y^2-(j-1)y]          1/2, all Z^k

For B0, Fidelity.Bridge.expB0_eq proves that the implemented integer
expression agrees with the printed half-integral-looking exponent
((2k+1)y^2-(2j-1)y)/2, including for negative y. The square-root cases use
integer powers of tau and the relation t=tau^2, rather than an undefined
choice of a square root in an arbitrary field.

FidelityLean/CoefficientBridge.lean proves equality of the independent and
implemented polynomials, coefficient functions, four kernels, affine
integrands, full sums, even-total-sum lattice, and weights. Actual declaration
names include Fidelity.Bridge.Edot_eq, dotCoeff_eq, ddotCoeff_eq,
lambdaKernel_eq, hKernel_eq, hPlusKernel_eq, hHatKernel_eq, jIntegrand_eq,
JK_eq, EvenLattice_eq, JKEven_eq and AffineFiniteSupport_eq.

FidelityLean/WeylBridge.lean proves the corresponding Weyl-object identities.
Its signsReindex equivalence reconciles signs indexed by output positions
with signs indexed by permuted variables. weylSummand_eq and
weylSum_numerator_eq identify the summands and finite orbit sums;
P_eq_weylSum and P_C0/P_Ct/P_BC/P_B0/P_Bminus/P_Bplus/P_Bt identify the seven
normalized objects. These bridge equalities use Lean's totalized field
operations and do not themselves assert regularity of an uncancelled quotient.

The certificate layer then proves the independent specification types using
these bridges. FidelityLean/Certificates.lean exposes the closed aggregate
acceptance types listed in Section 5. The conclusion cannot be supplied as
an additional hypothesis while retaining those acceptance types.

3. PROOF ROUTE AND CONDITIONS THAT REMAIN PART OF THE STATEMENTS

The development follows the paper's finite-to-affine determinant route:

  (a) Finite Weyl sums are converted into alternant and finite determinant
      ratios. Alternation, reflection, degree and normalization arguments
      support Lemma 2.1; the seven finite column families and their unit
      tail minors support Proposition 2.2.

  (b) The complementary-minor identity of Theorem 3.1 transfers between the
      finite determinant and the complementary kernel determinant. Its
      specification retains X*U=0 and transpose(Y)*Z=0 and all four nonzero
      selected-minor assumptions. The implementation includes the sign,
      Cauchy-Binet and top-form steps, rather than replacing this result by
      the desired final Hall-Littlewood identity.

  (c) Generating coefficients give the three folded kernel matrices and their
      complementary minors (Lemma 3.2). Folding and unfolding integer-indexed
      sequences, with finite-support and fixed-center accounting, yield
      Lemma 3.3.

  (d) Ordinary residue sequences produce C0/BC and B0/Bminus (Propositions
      4.1 and 4.2). The exceptional cases require the even-total-sum parity
      projection for Ct and the fixed-center or adjacent-column calculations
      for Bplus and Bt (Section 5). The half factors are retained.

  (e) Width zero is proved separately (Proposition 6.1). Classical determinant
      identities and seven zero-parameter specializations are then derived
      with the required cancellation and polynomial-specialization arguments
      (Corollary 6.2 and the additional zero-parameter certificate).

Relevant implementation modules include FiniteWeyl, ComplementaryMinors,
PaperTopForms, PaperCauchyBinet, Kernels, CoefficientMinors, Folding,
FiniteUnfolding, OrdinaryC0Identity, OrdinaryBCIdentity, OrdinaryBIdentities,
ExceptionalCtIdentity, ExceptionalBplusIdentity, ExceptionalBtIdentity,
WidthZero and PaperSpecializations, all under AffineDualJT/.
fidelity/route_requirements.json and fidelity/proof_route_paths.json record
the selected checkpoints and actual transitive dependency paths;
fidelity/proof_route.mmd gives the route diagram. The frozen report records
8 groups of route checks with 54 specified checkpoints. Dependency checks
establish that the named steps occur in the proof dependency closure;
correspondence with the paper's method is separately reviewed semantic evidence.

The main identities require n>=1. The displayed half-determinant identities
for Bplus and Bt require k>=1; their width-zero value is separately 1.
Proposition 6.1 covers all seven normalized width-zero values. Proposition
2.2, the positive-width kernel/minor interfaces and Section 4--5 determinant
applications retain their positive-rank and positive-width conditions.
Lemma 3.3 quantifies over arbitrary integer-indexed sequences in the paper's
field. Lemmas 5.2 and 5.4 retain k>0 and nonzero parameter hypotheses; the
symbolic indeterminate is proved nonzero at their paper entrypoints.

The Hall-Littlewood conclusions use the paper's rational-function field,
where the needed regularity and nonzero denominators are proved. Theorem 3.1
has its own arbitrary-field interface. An auxiliary general-field definition
does not make every raw quotient formula valid at all numeric poles: Lean
totalizes division, while a removable singularity is evaluated only after
the relevant cancellation or polynomial continuation has been justified.

Corollary 6.2 and its zero-parameter addendum assume n,k>=1. In Ct the
exponent k*y^2-j*y is zero at j=k,y=1, so a blanket claim that every nonzero
shift vanishes at t=0 would be false. The recorded proof retains this term
and uses the finite ratio and parity identity. The zero-parameter addendum
does not insert the right-side half factors into the left finite ratios.

4. EXISTING CERTIFICATE INTERFACE EXAMPLE

supplementary/ListingCheck.lean (relative to this edition or the outer bundle)
is the following complete existing interface example. It restates (1.3) with

example of the certificate interface already present in the release, not a
new proof development or a claim that Lean was rebuilt for this edition.
Nat, Int and Units F denote natural numbers, integers and the units of F.

  import FidelityLean.MainCertificates
  noncomputable section
  open Fidelity.Spec
  namespace PublicationExample
  variable {F : Type*} [Field F]

  def MainA13 {n : Nat} (k : Nat)
      (x : Fin n -> Units F) (t : F) : Prop :=
    P .B0 k x t 0 =
      JK (k := k) (2 * (k : Int) + 1)

  def StatementA13 (n k : Nat) : Prop :=
    0 < n -> MainA13 k (symbolicX n) (symbolicParameter n)

  example (n k : Nat) : StatementA13 n k :=
    Fidelity.Certificate.A13 n k
  end PublicationExample

7. FULL CHECKER COMMAND AND RECEIVER REPRODUCTION

The full audit entrypoint, from the extracted frozen project root, is:

  python3 scripts/check_fidelity.py

It creates a new run directory, compiles all mathematical project modules and
specification/certificate modules, exports their actual environment, checks
the axiom allowlist, independent acceptance types, bound review evidence and
route dependencies, performs the supporting experiments and mutation checks,
and reconstructs the project in two fresh directories. The checker records
commands, exit codes, timestamps, standard output/error and their hashes.
The resulting report is fidelity/runs/<new-run-id>/report.json.

An explicitly named new run may instead be requested as follows; choose a
name that has never been used in the receiving checkout:

  python3 scripts/check_fidelity.py --run-id receiver-check-001

The default per-command timeout is 3600 seconds. It can be changed using
--command-timeout SECONDS. A missing tool, unavailable dependency, timeout,
stale input or unresolved evidence gives a nonzero result. --static-only and
--skip-build cannot report full overall acceptance or fresh reconstruction.
Old pass labels and submitted logs are not substituted for new build evidence.

The following receiver procedure starts from the two files delivered under
formalization/ in the outer bundle. Commands are shown for a POSIX shell with
Python 3, Git, unzip, Elan and the required build tools available. The frozen
README.md also documents platform setup. On macOS, "shasum -a 256" may replace
"sha256sum" for manual hash calculations.

  sha256sum formalization/affine-dual-jt-fidelity-20261031.zip
